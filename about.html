STUDENT CONCEPT
Our world / Mission & backcasting

A bag for the life
you haven’t planned yet.

In 2035, access to electricity still exists. Access to a spare outlet is less certain. Our concept asks what a familiar everyday bag could become when people need to carry a personal power reserve.

OUR PROPOSED MISSION

Keep the bag useful.
Keep the day open.

We imagine Herschel evolving from carrying everyday essentials to supporting the energy those essentials need. Familiar design, adaptable components and repairable construction help one bag move through many versions of a life.

Familiar design.
A different kind of essential.

Herschel’s real brand is built around everyday bags and travel. Our project extends that design language into a new category: personal energy carry. This is a proposed evolution, not a real company strategy or historical product announcement.

SCENARIO ASSUMPTION

When charging comes with conditions.

During recurring supply restrictions, workplaces reserve outlets for equipment, cafés limit charging and public facilities allocate time. People leave home prepared and check their reserve before changing plans.

These restrictions are invented conditions for our 2035 world. Present-day research motivates the scenario; it does not establish that rationing is inevitable.

Backcasting / what must happen first

A future that has to be built.

Experiment now.

Prototype solar and motion systems. Measure real energy yield, added weight and comfort. Observe where people spend time and when they lose access to charging.

Build capabilities next.

Work with textile manufacturers and materials researchers. Run small outdoor pilots. Establish replaceable components, repair processes and realistic production costs.

Adapt over time.

Develop interchangeable collection components and transferable storage. Expand only when longevity, user value and manufacturing readiness support it.

A day shaped by reserve.

Morning

A landscape designer charges her phone before leaving. The plan: a project site, drawing work and dinner with friends.

Afternoon

Available sockets are reserved for site equipment. She prioritizes photographs, directions and calls rather than assuming she can recharge.

Evening

Before saying yes to dinner, she checks her remaining reserve. The backpack concept explores whether supplemental energy could keep that choice open.
