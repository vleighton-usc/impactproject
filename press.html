FICTIONAL PRESS RELEASE
March 12, 2035 · Product & design

Herschel introduces Energy Carry:
one familiar bag, two ways to collect.

A proposed Solar and Kinetic collection responds to routines shaped by uncertain access to charging.

Read the full announcement

VANCOUVER — In this imagined 2035 announcement, Herschel introduces Energy Carry, a collection built around a simple idea: a day should have room to change. The Solar model explores collecting daylight through exterior photovoltaic materials. The Kinetic model explores collecting energy from walking and load movement.

Both models are designed around a shared removable reserve module. The aim is to let people move stored energy between compatible bags and replace electronics without discarding the textile shell.

A material research partnership, imagined.

The proposed program would bring together a technical textile manufacturer, a photovoltaic integration laboratory and independent field-test partners. No organizations are named as confirmed partners. Published MIT and Nottingham Trent studies inform the material direction; they are not commercial endorsements.

Measured before promised.

Pilot testing would evaluate energy collected per day, exposure to shade, carrying comfort, abrasion and repair. The collection would be positioned as supplemental energy rather than unlimited power or a substitute for dependable infrastructure.

FICTIONAL PRESS RELEASE
June 4, 2035 · Care & circularity

Keep the bag.
Replace the part.

A proposed repair network treats energy components as serviceable parts of a long-lived bag.

Read the full announcement

VANCOUVER — In this fictional follow-up, Herschel outlines Reserve Care, a proposed support program for its Energy Carry collection. The program separates the life of the bag from the life of its power components.

The imagined network would connect bag repair workshops, trained electronics technicians and battery collection partners. Customers could have damaged ports diagnosed, replace worn collection panels and retire battery modules through an appropriate recovery route.

Shared components, fewer complete replacements.

A compatible reserve module could move from an outdoor backpack to a work bag as routines change. Design and manufacturing teams would need to validate connectors, electrical protections and environmental sealing before that promise could become real.

The program would also publish care guidance and distinguish cosmetic wear from faults requiring professional service. These services are proposed artifacts, not available offers or warranty terms.
