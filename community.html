INTERVIEW FEEDBACK / FROM TEAM SLIDES

What we heard.

These are early concept reactions documented in the presentation, not product tests or verified customer reviews. Participants had not used a working energy collecting backpack.

Alyssa Chey / Persona inspired by an interview

The outdoor enthusiast.

“I’ve done a lot of camping across the West Coast and something like a solar backpack that actually works and is not too heavy would be so handy.”

Her priorities: a light, breathable bag, organized compartments and gear that lasts. She wants her phone, headlamp and lantern useful across a camping weekend.

What this changes in the design

Measure the complete system’s weight against the backup batteries it could realistically supplement. Make ports replaceable and keep the bag aesthetically familiar. Do not assume solar collection eliminates spare batteries.

Ryan Jones / Persona inspired by an interview

The work traveler.

“It’s cool but will it actually charge when it’s cloudy? And if I’m in the office all day, I’m not getting sunlight or movement to generate power.”

His priorities: understated design, longevity and a phone that remains useful through commuting and travel. His questions challenge the amount of energy an ordinary day would collect.

What this changes in the design

Offer different collection approaches without promising equal output. Explain shade and indoor limitations, test comfort with a laptop and show energy yield rather than vague charging claims.

FICTIONAL COMMUNITY / 2035

The conversations around the bag.

An imagined discussion board explores new habits, disagreements and unintended consequences. Posts below are written as research probes, not real user endorsements.

r / EverydayReserve · sunroute_35 · Fictional post

Does anyone plan their walk around the sunny side of the street now?

I started taking the longer route after work. More daylight on the bag, fewer stops looking for a socket. But I’m wondering whether I’m changing my day for the bag instead of the bag helping my day.

Read an imagined reply

“Same question here. I’d rather know the actual energy difference before adding twenty minutes to my commute.”

This is why I wanted replaceable parts. I can keep the backpack I like, but only if a replacement connector is affordable and available.

r / EverydayReserve · desertreserve · Fictional post

People are trading charged reserve modules. Is that a service or a problem?

In our desert neighborhood, some people collect more solar energy than they use. An informal exchange has appeared. Who checks battery condition, who sets prices, and who gets left out?

Questions this artifact raises

Could portable energy become a small commodity? Could unsafe or counterfeit modules circulate? Would sharing improve access, or create another cost for people already facing energy insecurity?

Put the future in front of people.

Ask participants what they think this world means before explaining the product. Explore desirability, discomfort, rejection and unexpected reactions as well as whether the interface is understandable.

Interpretation

What would have to happen for this bag to feel ordinary?

Trade-offs

How much weight or routine change is acceptable for a little extra power?

Rejection

Would you rather carry a battery, change plans or reject this world entirely?
